# Focused mini-game research

Research was used to extract design principles, not to copy game content.

- **Downwell** — the creator interview on *#indiedev* discusses the game's origins and sound design. It reinforced the value of a compact action loop whose movement, danger, and reward all read together. Source: [Apple Podcasts, Downwell with Joonas Turner](https://podcasts.apple.com/us/podcast/downwell-with-joonas-turner/id1162362598?i=1000478676995).
- **A Short Hike** — Adam Robinson-Yu describes a deliberately tiny world, curiosity rewards, and a cohesive low-resolution art treatment. Mothlight uses a single readable garden arena, recurring light pickups, and one palette across sprites and UI. Sources: [developer post](https://blog.playstation.com/2021/08/05/crafting-a-tiny-open-world-a-look-behind-the-scenes-at-the-creation-of-a-short-hike/), [GDC postmortem listing](https://www.gdcvault.com/play/1026613/Independent-Games-Summit-Crafting-A).
- **Vampire Survivors** — Luca Galante's interview describes building the game with inexpensive tools and imperfect assets, and letting unusual combinations emerge during development. I applied the scope lesson (one small level, one enemy family, one core loop), not the game's auto-attack mechanics or visual identity. Source: [Game Developer interview summary](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/vampire-survivors-development-sounds-like-an-open-sourced-fueled-fever-dream).
- **Game feel research** — Pichlmair and Johansen group feel work into physical tuning, amplification/feedback, and support for carrying out player intent. That guided acceleration, movement slowdown while gathering, pulse feedback, hit invulnerability, and clear objective text. Source: [Designing Game Feel: A Survey](https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.09201).

## Applied design choices

Mothlight keeps the objective visible (21 lights / three petals), softens danger until the player has time to act, makes gathering reversible only before release, and answers pickups/deliveries/hits with animation and sound. Runs are short and repeatable so a player can learn the trade-off between carrying a larger bundle and using an early pulse for safety.
